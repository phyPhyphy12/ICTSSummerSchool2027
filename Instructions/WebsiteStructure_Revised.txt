Version: Revised brief
Starting point: https://phyphyphy12.github.io/ICTSSummerSchool2027/

ROLE
Act as a senior creative frontend engineer and product designer. You are working on the existing Summer School for Women in Physics (SSWP) 2027 website for ICTS-TIFR, Bengaluru.

PRIMARY INSTRUCTION — MODIFY THE EXISTING WEBSITE
Do NOT start a new project or replace the existing website with an unrelated template.
1. First inspect the existing repository, source code, assets, routes, dependencies, and the currently deployed website.
2. Treat the existing website as the starting point. Preserve useful working code, responsive behaviour, existing content that remains accurate, and the established deployment setup. And create a new github repo or branch as convenient for this final version.
3. Refactor and redesign the existing implementation in place. Do not discard the current project structure or change frameworks unless there is a clear technical reason.
4. Remove the fictional “ICTS Simulation HUD”, “Portal to Physics” facility narrative, “Lab Gateway”, “Holographic Core Corridor”, “Experiential Matrix”, invented telemetry, fake status indicators, and other simulation/game language.
5. Do not claim that ICTS has particular laboratories, instruments, equipment, facilities, experiments, mentorship ratios, or programme arrangements unless those details are explicitly confirmed for this year's school.
6. Do not invent dates, deadlines, application links, funding guarantees, accommodation details, speakers, experiments, or programme commitments.

CORE CREATIVE DIRECTION
Create a distinctive, editorial-quality, immersive website based on:
“HER CONTRIBUTIONS TO PHYSICS. YOUR FUTURE IN SCIENCE.”
The website should celebrate women physicists and their research, with a meaningful emphasis on Indian women scientists. Use a restrained, sophisticated visual language and a carefully choreographed scroll experience. The result should feel like a thoughtfully art-directed science exhibition / editorial feature, not a generic AI-generated sci-fi website.

The science-history experience is intended to inspire undergraduate women students to consider careers in physics. Do not turn the site into a physics textbook. For each featured scientist, prioritize:
- Her name.
- A real, verified portrait.
- Her research area / contribution in a concise phrase or sentence.
- One beautiful, scientifically grounded visual that represents her research.
Keep explanatory text short. The visual should carry much of the storytelling.

At least 50% of all scientists featured in the main “Women in Physics” journey/timeline must be Indian women scientists. Aim for exactly half or more; count the final published entries before delivery. Do not count organisers as featured scientists unless they are deliberately included in the scientist journey.

Verify each person's research and the wording of her contribution against reputable institutional, academic, museum, or scientific-history sources. Do not imply that a person single-handedly made a discovery when the work was collaborative or the historical record is more nuanced. Include a balanced mix of historical pioneers and contemporary researchers where appropriate.

For the final published set:
- Ensure at least 50% are Indian women scientists.
- Keep the same visual and editorial treatment for Indian and international scientists.
- Show the scientist's name, real portrait, research area/contribution, and a visual representation of the research.
- Keep text concise; do not add long scientific explanations.
- Provide unobtrusive source credits / “Read more” links where practical.
- Do not invent quotations, personal stories, awards, dates, or biographical details.

PORTRAITS, SCIENTIFIC VISUALS AND IMAGE INTEGRITY
- Use authentic photographs/portraits from reliable sources, preferably official institutional profiles, university pages, scientific societies, archives, or properly licensed repositories.
- Check image rights/licensing and provide attribution where required. Do not assume that an image is free to reuse just because it is online.
- Never generate AI portraits of real scientists. Never use an AI-generated image that could be mistaken for an actual scientist, historical photograph, laboratory, experiment, or research facility.
- Scientific visuals may be custom 2D/3D illustrations or animations, but they must accurately and honestly represent the relevant research idea.
- Label conceptual or illustrative visuals as “Conceptual visualization” or “Scientific illustration” where needed. Do not present them as actual experimental images or archival evidence.
- Do not use generic stock “scientist in lab” imagery, fictional ICTS facilities, invented equipment, or fake photographs of students.
- If a verified portrait or usable research visual cannot be found, omit that scientist or use a clearly labelled typographic treatment rather than fabricating imagery.

BRAND AND COLOUR — MUST MATCH THE ICTS LOGO
- Inspect the actual ICTS logo asset already present in the repository or obtain the official ICTS logo from an authoritative ICTS source.
- Sample the logo's actual primary colour(s) and build the site's colour palette from those values. The main accent colour must match the ICTS logo.
- Do not guess the brand colour and do not substitute a generic neon cyan/purple palette.
- Do not sample the TIFR logo as a substitute for the ICTS logo.
- Preserve the official logo artwork and its proportions. Do not recolour, distort, redraw, or add effects to the logo.
- Use the logo-derived colour consistently for links, buttons, timeline markers, focus states, and selected highlights. Use restrained neutral backgrounds and accessible contrast.
- Document the extracted colour values in CSS variables / design tokens so the palette is consistent.

VISUAL DESIGN / “NOT AI SLOP”
- Aim for a polished editorial / museum-exhibition design: strong typography, deliberate whitespace, excellent composition, restrained motion, and a clear information hierarchy.
- Avoid generic AI website patterns: excessive glowing gradients, random floating particles, fake glassmorphism dashboards, holographic panels, decorative equations with no context, meaningless data readouts, generic space backgrounds, excessive rounded cards, and overuse of neon.
- Avoid fake scientific UI labels, fake telemetry, fake performance metrics, fake “simulation active” indicators, and invented system messages.
- Use animation only when it supports the scientist's story or a real physics idea.
- Prefer a few exceptional transitions over constant motion.
- Make it feel credible for an academic research institute while remaining exciting and welcoming to undergraduate students.
- Use clear, natural language. Avoid inflated marketing copy and claims such as “world-class Nobel laureates under one roof” unless specifically verified and relevant.

SITE STRUCTURE AND CONTENT
Build a clear, navigable site with these sections. The immersive scientist journey may be the visual centrepiece, but practical programme information must remain easy to find.

A. HEADER / NAVIGATION
- Use a clean, responsive header.
- Include the SSWP 2027 identity and official ICTS logo.
- Navigation anchors: About the Programme, Women in Physics / Discoveries, Eligibility & Selection, Support & Accommodation, A Typical Day, Organisers.
- Include an Apply / Application button.
- Do not use sticky HUDs, telemetry, phase indicators, or fictional navigation labels.

B. HERO
- Main title: “Summer School for Women in Physics 2027”.
- A short, inspiring line about exploring physics and considering a future in science.
- Use a restrained visual that transitions naturally into the women-in-physics journey.
- Do not imply that the visitor is entering a virtual ICTS building or laboratory.
- Dates are not final. Do not invent or display dates. If needed, state “Dates to be announced”.
- The application action must clearly communicate that applications are not open yet.

C. WOMEN IN PHYSICS — THE MAIN IMMERSIVE JOURNEY
- Create a chronological or thematic scroll journey featuring selected women physicists and their research.
- Feature at least 50% Indian women scientists.
- Each scene should include the scientist's name, authentic portrait, concise research area/contribution, and a visual representation of her research.
- Keep the science explanation brief. The objective is recognition and inspiration, not a detailed lecture.
- Use a coherent visual transition between scenes; do not make each scientist a disconnected generic card.
- Consider a horizontal/vertical timeline, pinned scroll scenes, or a continuous visual motif.
- Ensure the section is usable on mobile and accessible without scroll animation.

Use concise, welcoming language. Do not overstate the programme or invent specific outcomes.
